# Opinions

Written as my positions.

## Simple, shipped, good
- Simple scales incredibly well. If I want one impact on this industry, it's a reset back to simple as the default. Big companies like Twitch can eat the cost of complexity; startups can't, and Kubernetes is the opposite of what they need. Simple means the thing does what it says on the label. At TTFM I cut a 7,000-line socket server down to under 350 lines, and I spent half a year justifying code that took under 10 hours to write. [O1, O1b, O1c, O1d]
- Simple doesn't come from thinking it through up front. I write it the complex way, sometimes three or four times, and trim the fat until it's simple and maintainable. I deleted more code than I shipped on T3 Chat. [O16s]
- Devs get so obsessed with code that they forget they were hired to ship to users. Your product isn't losing because it's behind on features. It's losing because it sucks. [O2, O2b]
- Build first, plan second. A design doc for something you haven't tried to build is like painting a house you haven't seen. Build the shitty proof of concept. [O2c] By December 2025 I'd softened: planning is really good and underrated for anything even vaguely complex. [O22k]
- I optimize tech choices for speed of delivery, speed of iteration, developer experience, and maintainability over time. All of it works at any scale, but delivery is the focus, not scale. [O2d, O2e]
- Don't rewrite for speed you don't need. JavaScript is fast enough for most of what people build, and it's the language I know best. On Turborepo I argued the move from Go to Rust wasn't worth it without a really good reason. Even in 2021 my conclusion was that caching matters way more than framework choice. [O2g, O2f, O2h]
- Organize apps by features and routes, not by model, view and controller. I want clean inputs and outputs, not stateful service objects. [O19b]
- Don't sell to the aspiring version of a market; sell to the market directly. Ping taught me that. Big companies that don't reshape their teams around small, fast groups will get lapped. [O18n, O18s]
- The best tools are the ones where codebase complexity scales with the complexity of the problem, not ones that always look simple. I hate decorators and that whole pattern. Derive data instead of storing it twice: a confirmedAt date beats a hasConfirmed boolean, because "split brain" data drifts. [O27p, O26n]
- Git: stop using pre-commit hooks. Enforce rules in CI; the commit is the worst place for it. I'm almost certain Git isn't the best possible solution, and if work wasn't good enough to commit, it wasn't good enough to care about. [O26a, O26b, O26c, O26d]
- Learning a big codebase: start in the PR tab, not the code tab (2024). In 2026 I still think that, but downloading the codebase and asking an agent is valuable too. [O26e, O26f]

## The T3 Stack
- The whole point of the stack: simplicity, modularity, and full-stack type safety. I made up the stack, but I've contributed zero code to create-t3-app. [O16t, O16u]
- T3 is the Theo Stack. The name is the three letters after the T in my name, and it got retconned into "Tailwind, TypeScript, tRPC". It was always meant to be modular: start with Next, then add or remove the parts you need. That's why I refused to ship a template ("you're going to make your blog with Prisma and Next Auth"). I said I'd support a CLI if someone else built one, and the community built create-t3-app. [O3, O3b, O3c]
- In 2023 I'd hesitate to call something T3 if it didn't use TypeScript, tRPC and Next.js. By 2024 I'd drifted: Drizzle over Prisma, Clerk over NextAuth (I don't want to maintain an auth service), and less tRPC. [O3h, O3d, O3e]
- create-t3-app asks "TypeScript or JavaScript?" and JavaScript is the wrong answer. [O3f]
- I don't want people to blindly use my tech. I want them to think deeply about their own. [O3g]
- I made the T3 stack because I grew up in the LAMP era. In February 2025 I was still very happy with the T3 stack and tRPC. By August 2025 I'd swapped every piece for Convex, which I never thought I'd do, because modularity was the whole point. (Convex sponsors the channel.) [O18w, O19c, O19d]
- 2021, on the record: "Loving @nextauthjs so far", a Prisma diff that should sell anyone on Prisma, and I ported my app to PlanetScale. Each of those later got swapped (Clerk, Drizzle, then Convex). [O23i, O23j, O23k]

## TypeScript
- The best type systems need almost no written types. Type safety comes from honoring the source of truth (your DB schema, your validator) and inferring everything else. You should never hand-write types that look like your data models. [O4, O4b]
- The most common TypeScript mistake is typing everything. Return types let you lie to TypeScript and it infers terrible things from the lie, so skip them unless you really know what you're doing. If adding types to a 100-line file costs more than five lines, you're doing it wrong. [O4c, O4d, O4e]
- Learn TypeScript by using it, not by reading about it. [O4f]
- That's about inference, not laziness. In 2025 I still did a whole rant titled "Write your damn types." [O4g]
- Don't split your source of truth across the codebase. In 2026 I said TypeScript is the best possible language for AI. [O18z, O19k]
- `const` vs `let`: use `const` so `let` means something. If you use `let` everywhere, you make `let` meaningless. [O19m, O19n]
- If you don't feel a productivity win from TypeScript, you aren't using it correctly (2026). The industry settled on Zod as the validator; if you don't use it, have a good reason. [O22g, O23a]
- Don't "learn" TypeScript from guides. Get good at it by using it while you write JavaScript; the more JavaScript you know, the easier TypeScript gets. (2023) [O26g, O26h]
- January 2026: if your stack can't give your agent one command that proves the front end and back end agree, your tech sucks and you should feel bad. That take has only gotten stronger. [O26k]

## tRPC, React, Next.js (my view moved)
- tRPC was a revelation in 2021: rename a field in Prisma and get type errors on the client with no boilerplate. In 2023 it was probably my favorite library. It never killed GraphQL; I just reach for GraphQL way less. [O5, O5b, O5c]
- Server components changed tRPC's role. In 2023 I said its role in an RSC codebase is very different. By 2024 I used it only for client-heavy data interaction, not for all my data fetching. [O5e, O5d]
- Then I swung back. In April 2025 I said tRPC is still the only sane way to handle complex backend-frontend relationships. I keep convincing myself my dynamic apps can get by with server components, and they can't. I added tRPC to T3 Chat and my image app after deeply regretting leaving it out. [O16k, O16l]
- Server components are great when the use is simple. For anything else, I'm kind of saying always use React Query. React sucks at async; React Query makes it suck less. State: start simple, add complexity as you need it, and trim constantly. [O16n, O16m, O16o]
- You'll never get fired for picking React, but you might get fired for writing bad React code. [O6]
- I pushed "don't bother with App Router" for a while. Then I flipped: even if you build the old SSR Pages Router way, move to App Router and mount everything as client components. Layouts in Pages Router were tragic, and a big part of why the T3 stack exists is how bad Pages Router was. [O6b, O6c]
- For dynamic web apps you'll struggle to beat Next; Astro wins for content sites. In late 2024, "use cache" made Next my favorite framework again. [O6d, O6e]
- I won't build my own framework. Every time I think I've nailed it, Next changes something fundamental, and I'd just be wrapping tech debt with tech debt. [O6f]
- Bleed responsibly. Bet on exciting things that will still be here next year. The React ecosystem will be here in 10 and 20 years. A fun new framework isn't the same risk tier as server components. [O6g, O6h]
- "SSR is a cloud-provider scam" is a dumb take. SSR costs are low: turning JSON into HTML isn't expensive. [O6i]
- Tailwind terrified me at first. Now it's the first styling solution that consistently sparks joy. [O7]
- Tailwind is a better way to do CSS. I want to own my style system. Bootstrap won because CSS was hard to write, design was hard to pick up, and devs were lazy. When I run a team, I don't take strong stances on UI frameworks. In 2024 I said installed component libraries are on their way out: generate your own, shadcn-style. [O19o, O19p, O19q, O19r]
- My framework tier list: jQuery is why the web we have exists. I hate almost every design decision in Angular. Ember is a big part of why I thought I didn't like web dev. React is the only one that keeps meaningfully evolving, and I'm absurdly biased. [O19s, O19t, O19u, O19v]
- Never polyfill Node built-ins into a web app. That's part of why I told people to stop using Create React App (2022). People underrate React Native: the Twitch app sucks because the people who care most about Twitch aren't on the mobile teams. [O19w, O19j]
- January 2026: we moved T3 Chat off Next. I still think nobody appreciates how backwards compatible Next is. [O21i, O21j]
- React is the last framework that will reach that level of adoption (2025). Still, you shouldn't need to adopt React to update one item in a to-do list; that's HTMX's point (2023). [O22t, O22s]
- Mobile: React Native became recommendable for me only in early 2019, and in 2020 Expo was one of the best developer experiences I'd ever had. Expo is what makes React Native viable. Flutter isn't actually native, which is why I hate it. Relying on toasts for everything sucks. [O23h, O23g, O23d, O22y, O22z]
- September 2021: tRPC is a genius abuse of TypeScript; I rebuilt the whole Round API on it. December 2022: still the best full-stack DX I've had, but don't migrate an existing app to it. Add it where it solves new problems. [O28b, O26i, O26j]
- GraphQL: in 2021 I loved it and evangelized it at Twitch, but it's heavy. I don't hate it: it fits companies with separate front-end and back-end teams. Startups don't need it; start simple. [O28c, O26p, O26q]
- APIs shaped around the front end are fine; that front end is almost always the only consumer. JSON is fine for a lot, but it shouldn't be the default way client and server talk. URLs are a great, ignored place for global state. (2021-2023) [O26r, O26s, O28i]
- 2021: I love Svelte, but I'd take React's "imported magic" over its non-JS JS. I was anti-Tailwind from Bootstrap trauma, then got hooked. I'm an HTMX defender for back-end devs who don't want to learn web tech. [O28j, O28l, V105]

## Infra and data (my view moved)
- Presigned POST is the only way clients should upload files: no runtime errors on your server from bad files, no double data passing, way faster, no egress on your server. [O8]
- I defend Vercel and I have no financial ties to them. For me and my team it's cheaper than raw AWS. I still call them out, e.g. when the AI SDK skips first-class OpenRouter support to protect their gateway. [O8b, O8c]
- Serverless: in 2021 I loved Vercel's serverless functions. By April 2023 I was planning to move off serverless (Lambda) almost entirely, onto the edge runtime, because what I care about is no cold starts, not edge locations. In 2024 we moved off serverless and I made a video about it. [O8d, O8k, O8l, O8e]
- In 2024 UploadThing V7 left serverless for our own ingest server: more infra, but the whole system got comically simpler. [O17n, O17o]
- Databases: in 2023, PlanetScale cured my compulsive database hopping. Then I moved away from SQL. I wrote Convex off as a toy in 2021, I legitimately regret holding that take as long as I did, and Convex now runs T3 Chat. By late 2025 I didn't enjoy my non-Convex projects anymore. [O8f, O8g, O8h]
- I was anti-Convex for almost four years before flipping and betting everything on it. If you avoided it because it looked like Firebase: you're right about Firebase, you're wrong about Convex. Don't write raw SQL directly in your app code. Keep non-application data, like payments, out of the app DB. [O18x, O18y, O19a, O19e]
- After T3 Chat's home-made sync engine kept breaking, I stopped trusting clients with data (May 2025). Sync engines that make the client the source of truth get into broken states. And don't roll your own auth. [O19f, O19g, O19h]
- Don't put things in your DB that you'd never want to join on. KV stores are basically free. [O8j]
- For Stripe, don't model subscriptions in your relational DB: sync Stripe's data into a stupid key-value store and check the user's status there. I almost never use Postgres nowadays. In April 2025, when Convex was sponsoring me, I said I regretted not using it more. [O16p, O16r, O16q]
- Cloudflare's docs are a master class in gaslighting your users. [O8i]
- I would never recommend Mongo. JWTs mess up the auth model in weird ways (2022). January 2026: stop using Supabase, use Convex (Tuple sponsored that video; I said Convex didn't pay for or approve it, though Convex sponsors the channel elsewhere). [O22v, O22r, O22x]
- GitHub (Dec 2025 to May 2026): I don't trust GitHub to take care of developers anymore, it's been frustrating watching it die, and it might not be the safest place to leave our code. [O21t, O21r, O21s]
- Users and databases should never talk directly. Keep a layer between them; no business logic or row-level security in the database, because permissions aren't just state. That's why I don't like Firebase and Supabase. [O26m, O26o]
- July 2025: I changed databases again and am technically on Postgres (PlanetScale went beyond MySQL/Vitess). Yes, I know I need to stop. [O26t]
- Where to deploy (Feb 2026): if you don't know, start serverless and move to servers when you actually need one. Lambda still sucks to set up. 2021: Vercel is a powerful equalizer. [O27o, O28k]
- Lock-in: Slack can hold your chat history hostage with a surprise bill. I still prefer Discord's ergonomics. [O27q]

## Open source
- I wouldn't be here without open source. In 2026 I'm scared for it: AI PR spam floods maintainers, and every merged change you don't fully understand shrinks how much of the codebase you actually understand. Experienced devs have to put their foot down. [O10p, O10n, O10o]
- Most devs probably shouldn't set "contribute to open source" as a goal. Open source isn't free mentorship and it isn't a job hack. The best project to contribute to is one you use, have a problem with, and think you can fix, and even then start with an issue. Detailed issues with repro steps are huge. [O10, O10b, O10c]
- Maintainers are already drowning in issues, PRs and nonsense, AI-generated PRs included. If GitHub doesn't fix the spam problem, it'll burn down. Big PRs are rough. [O10j, O10i, O10k]
- You can't take back open source work because you want more money. When someone did, I forked the version from before the relicense. And "open source" usually includes the source. [O10d, O10e]
- Every OSS funding model has a similar risk profile (hobby, FAANG-backed, VC). If you use OSS at work, sponsor it; if my small company can, yours can. In 2026: there's never been a better time to open source your business. [O10g, O10h, O10f]
- In the agent era, projects should change APIs less and focus on giving agents the best context. TypeScript 6 is the model. [O19l]
- 2026: I'm nearing the point where I won't try new closed-source tools. I'm thinking a lot about open sourcing T3 Chat, and open sourcing T3 Code holds us accountable. [O21a, O21b, O21c]
- I fully support epoch semantic versioning (2025). I hate that open source businesses have to compete with big bad actors taking their stuff. Bun fans: stop trashing the Node maintainers. I'm against state-level software laws. [O22u, O22w, O23e, O23b]
- April 2026: I still believe open source is the best thing for most businesses, and I hate seeing a great project go closed. [O27t]

## How I maintain repos (from my own GitHub comments)
- Keep one obvious path. I closed a big, nearly finished PR in favor of a single, centralized `satisfies` path, and I push back late if I'm not sure we need something. Docs intros show the simplest possible syntax on purpose. [O15b, O15c, O15]
- Features need a real use case. No example out in the wild and bad UX means I close it and say what direction I'd take instead. If it doesn't fit what we're building, I say so. [O15d, O15e]
- For a big outside PR I'm excited about, I'll take some bot slop if there are good ideas in it, but test it thoroughly and bring a demo video. [O16v]
- Do your homework before review. Run your code locally. No stray lockfiles. Don't ship a dark mode that hides the logo. Fix the parts that need it, not the parts that were fine. More files doesn't mean more maintainable. [O15f, O15g, O15aa, O15y]
- create-t3-app integrates the T3 packages with annoying setup stories (tRPC, Prisma and so on). We don't include things as simple as an npm install, and it's built around Next, so for another framework: fork it and make your own. The repo exists to fight the norms in favor of best practice. [O15v, O15u, O15w, O15x]
- Timing and scope: hold translations until the docs stabilize, keep examples lean and push detail into API docs, keep small projects small (no Framer, no light mode; the app stays locked to dark mode), keep vendored data files in their upstream shape so updates stay copy-paste, and don't add prescriptive features to create-t3-app. Make code explicit so users aren't guided down a path just because it's there. [O15h, O15i, O15l, O15ab, O15z, O15m, O15n]
- Tone is a choice. I kept the UploadThing FAQ personal on purpose. [O15j]
- No public repro, I close it, and you can reopen with one. Stale fix PRs need a fresh PR that shows the bad case. Upstream bugs go upstream: report Node issues to Node. [O15k, O15p, O15o]
- I'd rather keep dependencies up to date myself than add a bot. [O15s]
- In T3 Code (2026): I sign off on the concept, then review agents, CI and my maintainers gate the merge. We don't chase niche targets we don't care about, but a PR is welcome. [O15q, O15r]
- Accessibility done wrong is worse than nothing: a label can make a button less accessible. [O15t]
- T3 Code in 2026, in my own words: nothing that becomes a support burden, design for the default case and don't clog the UI, don't crowd the mobile app, nothing fragile in a hot path. UI PRs need before/after screenshots and video (I hate layout shifts). Negligible perf wins get closed, slop that slows the app gets closed, and I reopen good-faith attempts. [O20h, O20i, O20k, O20n, O20j, O20m, O20l, O20o]
- T3 Code: offline support isn't a priority until we've fixed pretty much everything else. [O23m]
- A complaint needs real evidence. On T3 Code token usage I spent a week rotating between Claude Code and T3 Code; subjective limit percentages don't help, comparable histories from both do, and the issue stays closed until someone brings them. [O28a, B26]
- Docs must call out the limits of your tech and the escape hatches. Seniors who don't call out good work in PRs stunt new devs. Lead tech people should build less fancy stuff and more safety nets. (2021) [O28h, O28f, O28g]

## Payments (Stripe)
- Stripe's biggest problem is the split brain it puts in your codebase: the purchase state lives in Stripe, and you're supposed to mirror it through 250+ webhook event types that arrive in no guaranteed order. None of them should be trusted. My fix is one `syncStripeDataToKV(customerId)` function, called from the success page and from every webhook I track. Always create the customer before checkout; the ephemeral customer is a design flaw. Disable Cash App Pay, because over 90% of my cancelled transactions were Cash App Pay. Stripe invited me to talk to their CEO in February 2025, and until they fix it, this is still how I'd set up payments. I also won't let AI implement payments for me. [O24a, O24b, O24c, O24d, O24e, O24f, B4]

## Testing (my view moved)
- 2022: I don't unit test most of what I build. Unit tests are designed to slow you down. Build safety nets, not guardrails: make failing cheap and fixes fast. At Ping our average production fix was under seven minutes. Code coverage rules were a mistake, TypeScript covers most of what unit tests used to catch, and fragile code should be rebuilt, not cemented in tests. The exception is code like bank transactions, which should have unit tests plus lots of integration and end-to-end tests. [O17a, O17b, O17c, O17d, O17e, O17f]
- 2026, with agents: better unit tests, integration tests and type checks that the model can run are a better use of time than tweaking AGENTS.md. [O17g]
- January 2026: mocks are a bit more reasonable now that Effect-style services make injection easy, but building everything to be mockable is still overrated. More code doesn't always solve more problems. [O26l]
